## A training file that cannot fail

*Illustrative numbers: this study is built for practice and is not the live case.*

The senior slides a second file across the desk. It records a study of 20 cases and 60 controls in which the exposure honestly does nothing. Six of the cases and 18 of the controls report it, 30 per cent on both sides. The true odds ratio is 1.00. Your task is to run this study badly, on purpose, and make it print a number that looks like a discovery.

<!-- STEP -->
## Four choices build the printed number

| Choice | What it decides |
|---|---|
| Control source | From the same workforce, from an office block's staff, or from the cases' relatives |
| The interviewer | Knows the hypothesis and works the ward, or is kept in the dark with one script |
| The past | What the men remember at interview, or what the employment records say |
| Matching | On age, on reactor work, or none at all |

Each choice moves the men in the table, and the odds ratio recomputes in front of you.

<!-- STEP -->
## Break it, then name what you broke

Make the file print an odds ratio of 2.00 or more from an exposure that does nothing. The exercise then asks you to tag each choice with the flaw it injects: selection, interviewer, recall or overmatching. The check passes only when the ratio and every tag are right, and its feedback works the cross-product and names the mechanism behind each choice. There is no timer; change any choice and check again as often as you like.
