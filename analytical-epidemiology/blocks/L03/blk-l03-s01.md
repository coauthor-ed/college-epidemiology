## Cold-case file 3: the men in the ward

The file opens on the patients rather than on the table. Thirty-three of the 35 men with lung cancer had smoked, and two had never smoked. Each of those 33 men had a cancer that doctors could rarely treat successfully in 1950.

In 1950 a London doctor published the answers he had collected from 35 men with lung cancer and 82 men without it. The table records what the two groups said about their smoking.

| Smoking | Cases (with lung cancer) | Controls (without lung cancer) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smokers, fewer than 5 cigarettes a day | 33 | 55 | 88 |
| Non-smokers | 2 | 27 | 29 |
| Total | 35 | 82 | 117 |

*Table 12. Doll and Hill, BMJ 1950, Table V. The controls were hospital patients with other diseases.*
