## A whole population lets both numbers be computed

The exercise on the next slide gives you a different kind of table: a whole population, with the exposure and the disease counted in everyone. That is what a cohort would have, so the relative risk and the odds ratio can both be worked out from the same counts and set side by side.

<!-- STEP -->
## One slider decides how common the disease is

The slider sets how common the disease is among the unexposed people, from rare to common, while the exposure keeps the same real effect throughout. As you move it, the relative risk bar stays where it is and the odds ratio bar slides away from it. The relative risk bar is the true effect, the same at every setting. The odds ratio bar is what a case-control study would report from the same people. The shaded band marks how close the two readings must be: within 10 per cent of each other. The task is done when you find a setting where the odds ratio sits inside that band, and the Check button tells you when you are there.

<!-- STEP -->
## Find the range yourself

Nothing on the screen names the rule. Slide the disease frequency up and down, watch which way the odds ratio moves, and note where the two bars come close. Check as often as you like, because there is no timer and no score.
