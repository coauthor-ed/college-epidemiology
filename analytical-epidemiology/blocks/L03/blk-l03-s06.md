## One word in the sentence is doing damage

The textbook reports that smokers had a risk of lung cancer 8.1 times that of non-smokers. The number 8.1 is right, and the word "risk" is wrong. Tap the word you would change.

<!-- STEP -->
## What the word should be

The number is an odds ratio: the odds of smoking among the cases divided by the odds among the controls. A relative risk of 8.1 would compare two risks directly, so if non-smokers had a risk of 1 in 1,000, smokers would have 8.1 in 1,000. The table cannot show either of those risks, because nobody counted the groups they would need.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why the number is still useful

Because lung cancer was rare in the men studied, the odds ratio sits close to the relative risk a cohort would have found. **An odds ratio is not a risk, but when the disease is rare it is a fair estimate of the relative risk.** The next slide asks how rare "rare" has to be.
