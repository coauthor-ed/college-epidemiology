## The gap is 27.2 points

The two exposure rates differ by 27.2 percentage points. A gap that size could still appear by luck, especially with only 35 patients, so the next question is how often chance alone makes a gap this big.

## Shuffle the labels to see what chance produces

The shuffle takes the 117 men and deals the labels "case" and "control" at random, keeping the two group sizes the same, then works out the gap again. Each deal is one world in which smoking has nothing to do with the cancer. Press the shuffle control many times and the gaps pile up as a histogram, most of them near zero and almost all within about nine points of it.

<!-- STEP -->
## The real gap sits outside the pile

Not one of the shuffles reaches 27 points. The source prints P < 0.001 for this table, which is the chance of a gap this big if smoking and lung cancer had no link. The usual cut-off is 0.05, and a result below it is called statistically significant. A gap the shuffles produce easily is marked n.s., not significant, which is what the DES file's other factors were.

<!-- STEP -->
## What a P value cannot say

A P value answers one question: is a gap this big unlikely to come from chance? It does not say that smoking caused the cancer, and it cannot see how the men were chosen or how they were asked. **A small P value rules out luck, not a faulty comparison.** The case still has to face the second question.
