## Two designs, two directions in time

In an analytical study each person is studied one at a time, but the conclusion is about the population those people were drawn from. The study can be run in two observational ways, case-control and cohort, and they start from opposite ends. The dots on the screen are people, drawn only to show the order of events.

<!-- STEP -->
## Two starting points

- A **cohort study** starts with people free of the disease, grouped by exposure, and follows them to see who becomes ill.
- A **case-control study** starts with people who already have the disease (cases) and people who do not (controls), and looks back for their exposure.

Use the switch to choose a design, then drag the time arrow. In the cohort the dots move forward from exposure to the outcome. In the case-control study you start at the disease and trace the dots back to the exposure. Nothing moves until you drag.

<!-- STEP -->
## Time and the direction of inquiry

Time always runs one way, from exposure to disease. In a cohort study the investigator also asks in that direction, from cause to effect, so the two arrows agree. Switch to the case-control design and the inquiry arrow turns round, because the questions run against time, from effect back to cause.

<!-- STEP -->
## Both ask the same two questions

**Both designs ask whether an exposure and a disease are associated and, if they are, how strongly.** They differ in where they start, and that difference decides what each design can measure. Lessons 3 and 5 return to this.
