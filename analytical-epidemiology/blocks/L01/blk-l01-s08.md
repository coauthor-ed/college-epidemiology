## Fourteen of twenty means nothing without a comparison

*Illustrative numbers.*

The plant's records come back: 14 of the 20 men with the tumour had cleaned the tanks. It is tempting to write "case solved" on the file, because 14 of 20 is 70 per cent.

<!-- STEP -->
## Read the gap in the table

Put the count in a table and the gap shows at once. The cases fill one column. The column where a comparison group should be is empty.

| | Men with the tumour (cases) | Comparison group |
|---|---|---|
| Cleaned tanks | 14 | ? |
| Did not | 6 | ? |
| Total | 20 | ? |

> Senior's note: So might 14 of any 20 men at that plant.

<!-- STEP -->
## The question for the next file

The note says that 14 of any 20 men at that plant could have cleaned tanks. If most of the workforce did that job, 70 per cent is what you would see even if cleaning were harmless. You now have a precise claim and one striking count, but nothing beside the count to compare it with. **Whom do you compare the 20 cases with?**
