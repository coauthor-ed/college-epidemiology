## Five parts of a testable claim

"Cigarette smoking causes lung cancer" was the tempting pick, and it is the textbook's own example of an incomplete hypothesis. It names a cause and a disease, but it says nothing about who, how much or how long, so no result could ever count against it. A testable claim states five things:

- **population**: the people the claim is about
- **cause**: the specific exposure
- **outcome**: the disease expected
- **dose-response**: how much exposure leads to how much disease
- **time-response**: how long it takes from exposure to disease

<!-- STEP -->
## Each part asks for data

The bare claim already carries two parts, the cause and the outcome. Tap the other three to add them. Every part you add puts a line on the list of data beside it. A claim that says "30-40 cigarettes a day" commits you to measuring cigarettes per day, and "after 20 years" commits you to measuring years of smoking.

<!-- STEP -->
## What if a part is missing?

Now take a part back out. Remove the time-response and "years of smoking" leaves the list, and with it any way to tell a 20-year effect from a 2-year one. **A part you leave out of the claim is a measurement you will never make.**
