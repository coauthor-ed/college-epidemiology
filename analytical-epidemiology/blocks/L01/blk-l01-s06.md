## Lenz looked backward, from babies to pills

Return to the 1961 file. Investigators traced 46 mothers who had given birth to babies with malformations and asked which drugs they had taken in early pregnancy.

> **41 of 46** mothers of affected babies had taken thalidomide.

Your textbook also reports a group of 300 mothers of normal babies, none of whom had taken it.

| | Mothers of affected babies (cases) | Mothers of normal babies (controls, as the textbook reports) |
|---|---|---|
| Took thalidomide | 41 | 0 |
| Did not take it | 5 | 300 |
| Total | 46 | 300 |

## Which way did this study look?

The study started from the effect, the affected babies, and looked back for the cause. On the flow you just used, its inquiry arrow points against time, so it is a case-control design. Laboratory experiments later confirmed that thalidomide damages the developing embryo.

## Check the file before you trust it

> Senior's note: The 41 of 46 comes from Widukind Lenz's 1962 enquiry in Hamburg, and accounts of that enquiry describe no formal control group. The 300 control mothers are your textbook's figure, and the accounts of the enquiry checked for this course do not mention them. A separate German study in 1962, by Weicker and colleagues, did use controls: 34 of 50 mothers of affected babies had taken the drug, against 2 of 90 control mothers.

*Sources: Lenz, Arch Environ Health 1962;5:100-105; Weicker et al. 1962 as summarised in a University of Pittsburgh Supercourse lecture (original paper not checked).*

What if the file held only the first column? You would still have 41 of 46, but no way to say whether that is high or simply how common the drug was among all pregnant women at the time.
