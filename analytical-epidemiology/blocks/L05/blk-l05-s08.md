## The records, assembled under your rules

*Live case: illustrative.*

The exercise you just did, scaled up, is the move this whole file has been building towards. The plant's personnel records yield a study cohort of 2,000 men with at least five years of tank cleaning. They also yield a control cohort of 8,000 other men from the same workforce, all free of the tumour when the follow-up opens. Over twenty years of follow-up, 12 of the tank cleaners and 4 of the other men develop the tumour. The records hold only men who entered the plant in those twenty years, so the cohort catches 16 tumours, not all 20 of your earlier cases: four older hands joined before the records begin.

## Two incidences, then the ratio

Each cohort earns its own incidence. Twelve cases in 2,000 men is 6.0 per 1,000 over the follow-up; four cases in 8,000 men is 0.5 per 1,000. Divide one by the other and the relative risk lands: 6.0 divided by 0.5 is 12.0. Beside it sits the number from lesson 3, the odds ratio of 13.2 computed from the ward's 20 cases and 60 controls.

## Two designs, one answer

Set the two ratios side by side, both measured from 1.0, the value that would mean no difference. The two numbers differ a little because one design counted odds among 20 cases and 60 controls, while the other counted incidence in 10,000 followed men. A very rare disease keeps the two estimates close. **The study that looked backward and the study that ran forward agree: tank cleaning and the tumour travel together.**
