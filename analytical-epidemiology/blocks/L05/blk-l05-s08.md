## The records, assembled under your rules

*Live case: illustrative.*

The exercise you just did, scaled up, is the move this file has been building towards. The plant's personnel records yield a study cohort of 2,000 men with at least five years of reactor cleaning. They also yield a control cohort of 8,000 other men from the same workforce, all free of the tumour when the follow-up opens. Over twenty years of follow-up, 12 of the reactor men and 4 of the other men develop the tumour.

## Two incidences, then the ratio

The scene posts the incidence each cohort earns. Twelve cases in 2,000 men is 6.0 per 1,000 over the follow-up; four cases in 8,000 men is 0.5 per 1,000. Divide one by the other and the relative risk lands: 6.0 divided by 0.5 is 12.0. Beside it sits the number from file 3, the odds ratio of 13.2 computed from the ward's 20 cases and 60 controls.

## Two designs, one answer

Select either ratio and it reads large under the bars, with the counts it was computed from; both bars start at 1.0, the value that would mean no difference. The two numbers differ a little because one design counted odds among 20 cases and 60 controls, while the other counted incidence in 10,000 followed men. A very rare disease keeps the two estimates close. **The study that looked backward and the study that ran forward agree: reactor cleaning and the tumour travel together.**
