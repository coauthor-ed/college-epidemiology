## Cold-case file 5: the long studies

The thickest file on the desk holds three studies that ran for years. British doctors on the Medical Register of the United Kingdom were followed from 1951. The residents of Framingham, a whole town, were counted year after year for heart disease. Uranium miners were watched for lung cancer, beside men who never went near a mine. On the first day of each study nobody in it had the disease being counted: the investigators wrote down who was exposed, and then they waited.

<!-- STEP -->
## What the waiting buys

Your own live case sits on the desk with its stamp. The odds ratio of 13.2 says how strongly tank cleaning and the tumour travel together, and the four bias checks say the number was not manufactured. What no case-control study can give is **incidence**, the number of new cases per head over time, because its groups were assembled after the fact and never followed forward. A cohort starts with people who are still well and follows them, so it can count exactly that.

> **The odds ratio says how strong. Incidence says how often.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Why follow thousands of well people

Case-control results were already in, so why follow tens of thousands of healthy people for years, at real cost, waiting for a few hundred to fall ill? Because the waiting produces the one number the other design cannot: the incidence in each group, and the ratio between them. All three studies share the same shape: each runs forward from its own start, with no end date fixed in advance, and the new cases appear one by one as the years pass.
