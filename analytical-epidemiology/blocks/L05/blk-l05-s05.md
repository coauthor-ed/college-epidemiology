## The answer, on a time line

If you said a retrospective cohort, the file agrees, and the name is worth unpacking, because retrospective here does not mean case-control. The study began in 1978, yet it ran from cause to effect like any cohort: it took the babies born at one hospital, split them by monitoring, and counted deaths. About 16,000 live births entered the published analysis, and the neonatal death rate came out 1.7 times higher in the unmonitored infants. The 1.7 is the crude ratio, computed straight from the counts.

> Senior's note: "The paper's own adjusted figure is 1.4 (95% CI 0.85 to 2.45), once the babies' own risk at birth is accounted for. A crude ratio can mislead; you watched one shrink in file 2."

## Move the start, keep the direction

The scene puts the named studies on dated axes, with the exposure and the outcomes as bands on each one. Slide the investigation-starts flag along the Boston axis to before the births began and the label changes to prospective cohort, because at that start the outcomes have not yet happened. Drag it back to 1978 and the label returns to retrospective, also called historical. The direction of inquiry stays pinned from cause to effect at every position, which is what separates all of these from a case-control study.

## A start in the middle earns a third name

Drop the flag inside the years when outcomes were still accumulating and the label reads a combination of retrospective and prospective. The chapter's example is Court-Brown and Doll. They assembled a cohort in 1955 of 13,352 patients who had received large doses of radiation therapy for ankylosing spondylitis, a disease of the spine, between 1934 and 1954. Deaths from leukaemia or aplastic anaemia already on record were assessed from the files, and the same cohort was then followed on from 1955 into the later years. The study found the death rate from these diseases substantially higher in the cohort than in the general population.

## Three names, one rule

Doll's doctors from 1951, the Framingham town study and the uranium miners all carry the same label for the same reason: their investigations started while the outcomes lay ahead of them. The Boston babies started after every outcome was over. Court-Brown started in between. Where the investigation starts decides the name; where the question runs, cause to effect, never changes.
