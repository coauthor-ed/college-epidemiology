## Two groups, nobody ill

*Illustrative numbers.*

The scene builds Table 16 out of people before it builds it out of letters. Strip one holds 200 exposed men, the group the chapter calls the study cohort; strip two holds 400 unexposed men, the control cohort. Every dot stands for 10 men, nobody in either strip has the disease, and so far there is nothing to count.

<!-- STEP -->
## Let the years run

Both groups are now followed forward under the same conditions over the same period, and a clock runs across the scene while it happens. Among the exposed, 20 men become cases; among the unexposed, 10 do. The strips were comparable people at the start, so a difference in what happened is exactly what this design exists to measure.

<!-- STEP -->
## Now the table can be built

With the outcomes known, the four cells of Table 16 drop into place. Cell a holds the 20 exposed cases, and b the 180 exposed men who stayed well. Cell c holds the 10 unexposed cases, and d the 390 unexposed men who stayed well. The margins a+b = 200 and c+d = 400 are the denominators of well people over time, the thing the case-control table in file 3 never had.

<!-- STEP -->
## Tap a row, read its incidence

Tap the exposed row and it posts a/(a+b) = 20/200, an incidence of 100 per 1,000 over the follow-up. Tap the unexposed row and it posts c/(c+d) = 10/400, or 25 per 1,000. The relative risk you met in file 3 as a formula is now a computation you have watched happen: 100 divided by 25 gives 4.00. **Incidence in each row first, then the ratio of the two incidences.**
