## The word on the file cover

A cohort is a group of people who share one characteristic or experience within a defined period of time. Everyone born in 2010 is the birth cohort of 2010. People exposed to one drug, vaccine or infection within a defined period form an exposure cohort, and men or women married in the same period form a marriage cohort. The word names the sharing, not the size of the group.

## The features that make the design

The studies in file 5 earn the name by three features. The cohorts are identified before the disease under investigation appears in them, the groups are observed over a period of time to measure how often disease appears, and the study proceeds from cause to effect. Because the design watches events that have not yet happened, the same study travels under other names: prospective study, longitudinal study, incidence study, forward-looking study. Cohort study is the name in widest use, and the one this desk uses.

## When the design is worth its cost

The method chapter sets four conditions under which a cohort is indicated. There should already be good evidence of an association between exposure and disease, drawn from clinical observation, descriptive work or case-control studies. The exposure should be rare but the disease common among the exposed, as in special occupational groups and people given X-rays. The loss of members during follow-up, which the chapter calls attrition, should be avoidable, because the cohort is stable, cooperative and easy to reach. And the funds should be ample, because following people for years is not cheap.

> Senior's note: "Cost is why a cohort comes late in an investigation, not last. You have earned it."
