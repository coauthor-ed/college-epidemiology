## The controls come back from the interviews

*Illustrative case: the plant, the city and every number are invented for this course.* Your rules picked 60 men from the same plant, free of the tumour and matched to the patients on age. Each of the 80 men, the 20 patients and the 60 controls, has now been asked the same question in the same way: did you ever clean the tanks?

<!-- STEP -->
## The answers fill the table

Fourteen of the 20 patients had cleaned the tanks, and 6 had not. Of the 60 controls, 9 had cleaned them and 51 had not. Those four counts are the cells of the table, a = 14, b = 9, c = 6 and d = 51, and the margins, 20 and 60, are the two groups you chose. Read each cell against its own group, never on its own: 14 is not "more" than 9 until you know that one is out of 20 and the other out of 60.

## One gap, and the question of its size

The exposure is far commoner among the patients than among the men like them who stayed well. That gap is what the file has been working towards. But a gap between two shares is not yet a number that can be reported or set beside another study. **The table says there is a difference. It does not yet say how large.** The next lesson turns these four counts into that one number.
