## The controls come back and the table fills

*Illustrative numbers.*

Sixty men from the same plant, matched to the patients on age, have been interviewed, and 9 of them had cleaned the reactors. The case file now holds the filled 2x2: 14 exposed cases, 9 exposed controls, 6 unexposed cases and 51 unexposed controls.

## Read the filled table

| Reactor cleaning | Cases | Controls |
|---|---|---|
| Cleaned reactors | 14 (a) | 9 (b) |
| Did not | 6 (c) | 51 (d) |
| **Total** | **20** (a+c) | **60** (b+d) |

The letters a to d name the four cells, and the margins a+c and b+d are the two groups you chose. Read the cells against each other rather than one at a time, because a single count on its own says nothing.

## One gap, and the question of its size

Fourteen of the 20 patients had cleaned reactors, against 9 of the 60 controls, so the exposure is far commoner among the cases. That gap is what the file has been working towards, but a gap between two shares is not yet a number that can be reported or set beside another study. **The table says there is a difference. It does not yet say how large.** What does this table say, and how strongly?
