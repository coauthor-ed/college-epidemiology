## Cold-case file 2: eight young women

*The age curve in the graph is an illustrative shape, drawn to show the usual pattern of this cancer. It is not measured data.*

Between 1966 and 1969, doctors in Boston saw eight young women with adenocarcinoma of the vagina, a cancer of the vagina's lining that is usually found in women past 50. Seven of the eight were patients at one hospital, and that is what turned a few separate cases into a file worth opening. A cancer this rare should appear once in a lifetime, not eight times in one city in three years.

<!-- STEP -->
## Their ages sit where the curve is almost flat

On the graph, the usual number of new cases climbs steeply after age 50 and stays close to zero before 40. All eight women sit at the far left, in the flat part of the curve. **A cancer of later life had appeared eight times in teenagers and young women.**

## Why the study cannot wait for new cases

The obvious next step would be to follow healthy young women forward and count who develops the cancer. The wait rules that out, because new cases are so few that a study would need decades to collect a handful. The eight women already exist, and their early lives are on record. So the investigation runs backwards from the eight, asking what was different about their past.

**When the outcome is rare, you start from the people who already have it.** Whose past should you compare theirs with?
