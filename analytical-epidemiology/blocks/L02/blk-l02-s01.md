## Cold-case file 2: eight young women

Between 1966 and 1969, doctors in Boston saw eight young women, the oldest of them 22, with adenocarcinoma of the vagina, a cancer of the lining of the vagina. Seven of the eight were treated at one hospital. Each woman was one patient on a ward, but together they were a pattern that nobody could explain, because this cancer belongs to a quite different stage of life.

<!-- STEP -->
## Their ages sit where the curve is almost flat

The usual pattern of this cancer climbs steeply after age 50 and stays close to zero before 40. Placed on that curve, all eight women sit at the far left, between 15 and 22, in the flat part where new cases should almost never appear. **A cancer of later life had appeared eight times in teenagers and young women, in one city, in three years.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Why the study cannot wait for new cases

The obvious study would follow healthy young women forward and count who develops the cancer. Follow ten thousand of them for twenty years and you may see one case, perhaps none, so the answer would take a lifetime. The eight women already exist, and their mothers' pregnancies are on record. So the investigation turns round and runs backwards from the eight, asking what was different about their past.

**When the outcome is rare, you start from the people who already have it.** Whose past should you compare theirs with?
