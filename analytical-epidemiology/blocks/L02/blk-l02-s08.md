# Exercise: Pick 60 fair controls

<!-- STEP -->
## Your turn to choose the control group

*Illustrative case: the plant, the city and every number are invented for this course.*

The live case has 20 patients and needs 60 controls, three for each patient. The plant's personnel office sends a list of men who worked there, with each man's age, job and medical record.

**A control group is fair when the only thing that separates it from the cases is the disease.**

<!-- STEP -->
## How the exercise on the next slide works

On the next slide you set the rules the personnel office uses to pick the 60 controls. Three rules can be switched on or off: leave out men with the tumour in their record, match each patient's age, and match each patient's job. Every time you change a rule, the 60 men it would pick are drawn beside the 20 patients, with how many have the tumour, how old they are and how many cleaned the reactors.

Look at the last of those numbers as you work. A fair rule leaves it free to come out however it comes out, because it is the very thing the study measures.

**Press Check when the rules give a group that differs from the patients only in the disease.** The feedback names any rule that is wrong and shows the number that gives it away. You can try again as often as you like.
