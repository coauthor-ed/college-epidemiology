## Fix what counts as a case before you look

A case is not simply anyone who has the disease. The diagnostic criteria and the stage are written down before the study starts, and they are not changed while it runs. If the definition drifts halfway through, you cannot tell a real difference from a change in who was allowed in.

<!-- STEP -->
## Take newly diagnosed cases, not old ones

A patient diagnosed within a fixed period is more useful than one who has lived with the disease for years. Old cases are the people who survived long enough to be found, so they may differ from the patients who died soon after diagnosis. Taking the whole series from one hospital is convenient, but cases from two hospitals come closer to all the cases in the city.

## Where the cases come from decides who is missing

A hospital series misses anyone who never reached that hospital, while a population registry reaches everyone in a defined area. Neither source is always better, because a registry may not exist for a rare tumour and a hospital series at least keeps records. State plainly which source you used, because the controls must come from the same place.

**Cases from one place and controls from another can differ for reasons that have nothing to do with exposure.** That is the problem the next step has to solve.
