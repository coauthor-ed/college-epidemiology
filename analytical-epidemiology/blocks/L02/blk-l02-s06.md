## Drinking looks like a cause of oesophageal cancer

*Illustrative numbers.*

In a case-control study of alcohol and oesophageal cancer, 60 of the 100 cases drank alcohol against 40 of 112 controls. Sixty per cent against 36 per cent looks like a real difference, and it is easy to stop there.

<!-- STEP -->
## Split by smoking and the difference disappears

Smoking is commoner among drinkers than among non-drinkers, and smoking is itself a risk factor for oesophageal cancer. Split the table into smokers and non-smokers, then compare drinkers with non-drinkers inside each group. Among smokers, 50 of the 55 cases and 20 of the 22 controls drank, which is 91 per cent on both sides. Among non-smokers, 10 of the 45 cases and 20 of the 90 controls drank, which is 22 per cent on both sides.

**The difference you saw was smoking travelling with the drinkers, not alcohol acting on its own.**

## Give the problem its name, and match it away

A factor like smoking here is a **confounding factor**: it is associated with the exposure, it is itself a risk factor for the disease, and it sits unevenly in the two groups. Age does the same in a study of the contraceptive pill and breast cancer, because the women taking the pill were younger and younger women are less likely to develop that cancer.

**Matching** is the design's answer. You choose controls so that the distribution of age, or of smoking, is the same in both groups. Group matching fixes the same proportion in each band on both sides, and pair matching gives every case a control of the same age and sex.

<!-- STEP -->
## Two ways matching goes wrong

Do not match on the suspected cause. If cases and controls are matched on pill use, every pair is alike on the very thing under test and the study can no longer see its effect. Overmatching is the second mistake: match on too many things, or on something the exposure itself changes, and suitable controls become hard to find while the difference between the groups is pulled down toward nothing.

**Match on what could confound the comparison, never on what you are trying to measure.** The next file is a study that matched its controls well, and it found its cause.
