## The eight women return, with 32 controls

The eight women are the cases. For each of them, the investigators found four girls born in the same hospital within days of her who had not developed the cancer: 32 controls. The mothers' birth records describe their pregnancies, and four facts about each pregnancy became the suspects in a lineup, to be checked in the cases and the controls in exactly the same way.

<!-- STEP -->
## Three suspects that do not separate the groups

The mothers of the cases were 26.1 years old on average, against 29.3 for the controls. Seven of the eight case mothers smoked, against 21 of the 32 control mothers, and 1 against 4 had X-rays during pregnancy. The study marks all three differences n.s., not significant: gaps this size could easily come from chance. The next lessons explain how such a judgement is made.

<!-- STEP -->
## One suspect separates them completely

The last suspect is oestrogen. **Seven of the eight case mothers had been given diethylstilbestrol, or DES, in early pregnancy, and none of the 32 control mothers had.** A gap that large is very unlikely to come from chance: the study marks it P < 0.00001. Ten to twenty years after the exposed pregnancy, the daughters developed the cancer. Later cases confirmed the association, and DES was withdrawn from use in pregnancy.

> Senior's note: DES was prescribed by doctors to prevent miscarriage. Nothing in this file is a criticism of the mothers.

**A comparison is only as fair as its controls, and these controls were fair enough to reveal the cause.**
