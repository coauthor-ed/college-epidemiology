## The cold case comes back, as a lineup

The eight women are the cases. For each one, the investigators found four girls born in the same hospital at about the same time who did not develop the cancer, and those 32 are the controls. The birth records also describe four things about each mother's pregnancy, so each one becomes a suspect in the lineup, one row of the table.

## Open each suspect and compare the two groups

Select a row to open it. Maternal age comes out at 26.1 years for the cases against 29.3 for the controls, and the study marks that difference n.s., not significant. Maternal smoking is 7 against 21, and antenatal X-rays are 1 against 4; the study also marks both of them n.s. The next file explains what "not significant" means.

## One row separates the two groups

The fourth row is oestrogen exposure. Seven of the eight cases' mothers had been given diethyl-stilbestrol, or DES, in early pregnancy to prevent a miscarriage, and none of the 32 control mothers had been given it. A gap that large is very unlikely to come from chance, and the study marks it P < 0.00001. Ten to twenty years after the exposed pregnancy, the daughters developed the cancer.

> Senior's note: the drug was prescribed to prevent miscarriage. Nothing in this file is a criticism of the mothers.

**A comparison is only as fair as its controls, and these controls were fair enough to reveal the cause.** Later cases confirmed the association.
